# Presenting style (house voice)

Canonical presenting craft for Jumbo AI training videos. Instructional
design still owns *what* must land (`SKILL.md`, Learning Brief). This file
owns *how it should feel when spoken and shown*: calm, precise, visually
led explanation that builds intuition before jargon - not a brochure, not
a hype walkthrough, not a compliance lecture.

**AI Foundations override:** beat order, timing, and hard limits live in
[`courses/notes/jumbo-ai-video-structure-guide.md`](../../../courses/notes/jumbo-ai-video-structure-guide.md)
(skill pointer:
[jumbo-video-structure.md](jumbo-video-structure.md)). That guide **wins**
over any conflicting beat advice below (no agenda path, concept before
name, three-line recap then stop, keep "in the wild").

**Reference study:** 3Blue1Brown,
[But what is a neural network?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aircAruvnKk)
(Deep learning chapter 1, ~18 min). Steal the *craft*: wonder-led open,
visual build, honest limits. Do not copy academic length or math depth
into a ~5–6 minute workplace module.

For script critique against this style, use **`video-review-authenticity`**.

## Feel in one line

A thoughtful guide sitting beside you at a diagram, making the structure
feel motivated - not a salesman demoing tools, and not a narrator reading
slides.

## What the reference does well (transferable)

### 1. Wonder hook on a concrete thing

Opens on a recognisable object and invites appreciation of a paradox.
Curiosity first, branding never.

**For us (Foundations):** open on a plausible meeting moment the learner
has been in. Name the discomfort honestly. End with an implicit promise.
Never "Welcome to module one…" and never "in this video we will cover".

### 2. Orient without an agenda

Older drafts asked for a spoken "purpose + 2–3 beat path" after the hook.
**Do not do that for Foundations.** The hook's promise plus the concept
beat *is* the orient. An agenda dump is banned by the structure guide.

**For non-Foundations training** (if any): a one-sentence purpose is fine;
never a TOC slide read aloud.

### 3. Assume no background; demystify the buzzword

Treat every jargon word as something to earn. Prefer "what it actually is"
over buzzword stacks.

### 4. Concept before vocabulary (non-negotiable for Foundations)

Explain in words the learner already has. **Zero new terms in the concept
beat.** Attach the label only after the idea is sitting in working memory.
Every analogy needs "where it breaks" in the same breath.

### 5. Motivate the structure before the mechanism

Before labels get dense, say what we hope the thing is doing in human
terms. Motivation first, names second.

### 6. Visual-led teaching (VO narrates the picture)

Almost every beat is tied to a changing diagram. Speech points at what
you see. Build diagrams progressively. Weed seductive details.

### 7. Honest limits

Name limits that protect the learner (fluency is not truth; check before it
leaves your desk). Admit when a teaching model is simplified on purpose.

### 8. Calm invitational voice

"You'll notice", "here's the thing". Warm, precise, unhurried. Contractions
are fine; adrenaline is not. Second person throughout.

### 9. Recap as three lines, then stop

Foundations: concept in plain words / the term / the behaviour. Spoken and
on screen. **No** "see you in the next video", no music sting. The last
thing they hear is the thing you want them to keep.

### 10. One primary throughline per video

One idea. If the takeaway needs an "and", split the video.

## What not to copy

| Reference habit | Why it fails here |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| 15–20 minute single-topic deep dive | Foundations default ~5–6 min; hard cap 8 |
| Heavy math as the payload | Workplace literacy, not a linear-algebra course |
| Patreon / subscribe mechanics | Not our format |
| Soft series outro rituals | Close on the three-line recap |

## What we previously dropped (do not regress)

Earlier drafts leaned toward high-energy tool-demo YouTube craft, or toward
an agenda-style structured intro. Both are **out of house style** for
Foundations. Prefer calm visual explanation, meeting-moment hooks, and
concept-before-name.

## House beat format (Foundations)

Use the six-beat sheet (or the short / scenario / compliance /
vocabulary-cluster variants) from the structure guide. Do not invent a
parallel shape.

| Beat | Presenting job |
|------|----------------|
| Hook | Meeting moment + discomfort + implicit promise |
| Concept | Plain language; zero new terms; analogy + limits |
| Name it | Term, pause, on screen, callback definition |
| In the wild | Real colleague sentence; unpack; misuse note |
| So what | Judgement / trap / question / risk |
| Recap | Three lines; stop |

## Script voice checklist (write / rewrite)

Scripts should pass these by ear (read aloud):

- [ ] Opens on a meeting moment / concrete tension, not module chrome
- [ ] No agenda dump; no definitions in the hook
- [ ] Zero new terms until Name it; ≤4 new terms total
- [ ] Calm, precise, invitational ("you" / "we"); not hype, not brochure
- [ ] Analogy followed by where it breaks
- [ ] VO clearly points at something showable on screen
- [ ] `[PAUSE]` marked after every new term and before the recap
- [ ] So-what is a specific behaviour, not "now you understand X"
- [ ] Recap is exactly three lines; then stop
- [ ] No brochure verbs ("leverage", "empower", "embark") unless quoting policy
- [ ] Australian English; no em dashes
- [ ] Read aloud start to finish without stumbling

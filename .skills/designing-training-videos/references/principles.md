# Principles: evidence base for training video design

Short reference for agents. Prefer applying the checklists in
`SKILL.md`; open this when you need the "why" or a sharper cut decision.

## What good corporate training video looks like (2025–2026 practice)

Industry guidance converges on the same pattern:

- **Modular** - one objective per clip; long topics become a series
- **Short enough to finish** - often 2–5 minutes for a single workflow;
  3–8 minutes for a concept lesson; avoid 20–45 minute lecture dumps
- **Script-first** - write what must be learned, then visuals
- **Task- and decision-shaped** - show what to do, not org-chart tours
- **Reinforced** - quiz, discussion, or on-the-job prompt after watching
- **Maintainable** - one idea per module so policy changes do not force a
  full re-record of a course

## Mayer's multimedia principles (apply hard)

From [Mayer's 12 principles](https://www.digitallearninginstitute.com/blog/mayers-principles-multimedia-learning)
and related instructional-video research. For training explainers, weight
these highest:

| Principle | In practice for this skill |
|-----------|----------------------------|
| **Coherence** | Delete decorative AI graphics, side tangents, and "while we're here" |
| **Signalling** | Highlight the must-land with type, colour, motion, or callout - one focus |
| **Segmenting** | One teaching idea per beat; split episodes rather than overload |
| **Multimedia** | Words + purposeful visuals; not slides of paragraphs |
| **Modality** | Prefer narration + visuals; keep on-screen text short labels |
| **Redundancy** | Do not make viewers read a full script while hearing it |
| **Temporal contiguity** | Show the thing while you say the thing |
| **Personalisation** | Conversational "you / we"; not policy-robot voice |
| **Pre-training** | Teach terms before using them in a workflow video |
| **Voice** | Clear human (or high-quality humanlike) VO; consistent series voice |

House presenting craft (wonder hooks, structured intro, calm visual build):
[presenting-style.md](presenting-style.md). Critique scripts with
`video-review-authenticity`.

Talking-head as the only visual rarely teaches better than diagrams and
examples. Use a host face sparingly for trust; teach with graphics.

## Cognitive load: the real constraint

Learners have limited working memory. Training fails when we:

- Teach three frameworks in one video
- Put dense text on screen while narrating different dense text
- Add novelty (jokes, transitions, stock metaphors) that does not carry meaning

**Fix:** lock must-land first, then budget beats, then add engagement that
serves those beats.

## Importance delivery (the point of this skill)

Every video has a **success condition**: the viewer can state or apply the
must-land without rewatching.

Operationalise it:

1. Write must-land as learner-facing sentences ("AI predicts likely next
   words; it does not understand like a person").
2. Teach each must-land **explicitly** on screen (label or diagram) and in
   VO (say it cleanly once, then apply it).
3. Recap must-land in the closing 20–40 seconds in quotable form.
4. Test must-land in the quiz/discussion.

If a beautiful scene does not serve a must-land bullet, cut it.

## Bloom-ish verb guide (keep objectives honest)

| If you want… | Use verbs like… | Avoid faking with… |
|--------------|-----------------|--------------------|
| Awareness | Recognise, name, list | "Understand AI" |
| Comprehension | Explain, distinguish, summarise | "Know about…" |
| Application | Apply, choose, draft, escalate | "Be aware of…" |
| Judgment | Decide, critique, verify | "Appreciate…" |

Corporate training usually needs **explain** or **apply**. Reserve
"understand" only if you immediately define the observable behaviour.

## Accessibility and inclusion (minimum bar)

- Captions by default for LMS / muted playback
- Do not rely on colour alone for myth vs fact or do vs don't
- Plain language; expand acronyms on first use
- Avoid idioms that exclude non-native speakers when a plain phrase works
- Respect reduced-sensation design: no seizure-risk flicker

## Fun without fluff

Modern business training can be warm, sharp, and occasionally funny.
That is not the same as entertainment-first content.

**Allowed:** surprising contrast, honest workplace examples, dry wit,
"wrong then right" reveals, human stakes.

**Not allowed:** content that exists only to be liked; mockery of
learners; meme literacy tests; hype about AI replacing everyone.

Energy comes from **clarity + relevance + rhythm**, not from louder
graphics.

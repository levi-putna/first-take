# Tempo and engagement

How corporate training videos should feel in time - and how to keep a
modern enterprise audience watching without turning the lesson into a
sketch show.

## Tempo bands by video type

| Type | Target length | Teaching beats | Notes |
|------|---------------|----------------|-------|
| Micro workflow | 90s–3 min | 2–4 | One task; end when the task ends |
| Concept lesson | 4–6 min | 4–6 | Literacy / mental models |
| Full training episode | 5–8 min | 5–7 | Series episode; still one primary objective |
| Deep dive (rare) | 8–12 min | Split preferred | Flag cost/attention; usually better as two episodes |

If Gate 1 production will exceed ~5 minutes of narration, flag audio
generation cost to the user (see `video-generate-explainer`).

## The first 15 seconds

Viewers decide quickly. Frame one should already show a relevant tension
or question.

**Strong hooks**
- A concrete object + paradox ("This draft sounds perfect. The figure is wrong.")
- A familiar workplace snag with quiet tension
- A plain promise after wonder ("In six minutes you will be able to explain X without jargon.")

**Weak hooks**
- Logo only for 3+ seconds
- "Welcome to module four of our AI enablement journey…"
- Abstract mission statements with no workplace or concept object
- High-energy demo adrenaline that fights the calm explainer house style

After the hook, land a **structured orient** (purpose + 2–3 beat path)
within ~45–60s. Presenting craft:
[presenting-style.md](presenting-style.md).

## Speakable pacing

Approximate VO budgets (natural corporate explainer pace):

- ~130–150 words per minute for clarity-first training
- Dense definitions: slow slightly; leave a beat after the key sentence
- Lists: one item → visual change → next item (do not machine-gun five)

Script for the ear. If a sentence is hard to say aloud, rewrite it.

## Visual rhythm

Align with Mayer's temporal contiguity:

- New spoken idea → new visual focus
- Hold a diagram long enough to read labels (often 3–5+ seconds)
- Use sparse motion: enter, emphasise, exit - not constant float
- Pattern interrupt once or twice max (e.g. flash a wrong answer, then correct)

For Remotion productions, prefer frame-driven emphasis on the focal
element only (see project `DESIGN.md` and the explainer skill's continuity
rules).

## Energy curve (5–8 minute episode)

```
High  |  Hook!
      |     \          contrast / scenario bump
Med   |      \________/^^^^^^^^\____
Low   |                        recap (clear, not sleepy)
      +----------------------------------------------→ time
```

- Start relatively high energy (stakes)
- Settle into steady teaching (clarity > hype)
- One mid-video bump (scenario, myth, or decision)
- Recap: calmer, sharper, quotable - not a second lecture

## Engagement palette (pick few)

| Technique | Use when | Watch-out |
|-----------|----------|-----------|
| Workplace vignette | Need relevance fast | Keep it one beat, not a short film |
| Myth / fact | Misconceptions block learning | Cap at 3 myths per video |
| Do / don't | Behaviour change | Make don'ts specific and actionable |
| Wrong → right | Overconfidence risk | Show the wrong briefly, then correct clearly |
| Decision fork | Judgment skills | One fork; resolve it |
| Analogies | Abstract tech ideas | One analogy; drop it if it fights the model |
| Dry wit | Tone and memory | Never punch down; never obscure the point |
| Progress / chapter pills | Series navigation | Do not eat the hook |

## Engagement anti-patterns

- Opening wonder/tension that never pays off in the teach
- Three analogies for one idea
- Background music under definition-heavy VO
- "Fun" side quests that are not assessed and not must-land
- Sarcasm about "people who fear AI" (creates shame, reduces adoption)

## Reinforcement timing

- **In-video:** one "try this mentally" prompt mid-way for longer lessons
- **End-of-video:** 15–30 second recap of must-land only
- **After video:** quiz / discussion in LMS or facilitator notes - designed
  in this skill, not improvised at render time

## Series tempo

Across a course:

- Episodes 1–2: slower, more quotable, glossary-friendly (rewatch assets)
- Middle episodes: slightly brisker; more examples and application
- Final episode: horizon + mindset; lighter cognitive load; clear CTA to
  the next course

Keep intro/outro chrome consistent and short so teaching time is protected.

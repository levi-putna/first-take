# Structure: Scenario / judgment

**Canonical variant:** Section 7 of
[`courses/notes/jumbo-ai-video-structure-guide.md`](../../../../courses/notes/jumbo-ai-video-structure-guide.md)

Use when the goal is **behaviour change** under ambiguity - escalate vs
proceed, trust vs verify, paste vs protect. Better for habits than for
concept transfer.

Program fit: Foundations **3.4**, **6.4** (and similar).

## Shape (Foundations scenario variant)

| # | Beat | Job |
|---|------|-----|
| 1 | Hook | Drop into a recognisable situation |
| 2 | Scenario | Walk the situation as it unfolds |
| 3 | The moment it goes wrong | Freeze on the failure / decision point |
| 4 | The principle | Extract the portable rule |
| 5 | Name it | Attach the term / rule label if needed |
| 6 | Recap | Three lines. Stop. |

For **compliance** videos (6.1–6.3), add a seventh beat before Recap:
**The rule, stated flatly** - verbatim, on screen, unmissable. Keep the
escalation contact card visible through the recap.

## Beat writing tips

- Scenario must feel like their week, not a cartoon villain.
- Teach **criteria** ("If it includes player PII, stop") more than a single
  scripted answer.
- Invert the default: principle after the failure moment, not before.
- Close with a principle they can reuse tomorrow - no soft sign-off.

## On-screen text pattern

- Scenario title chip
- Decision: A / B (or Do / Escalate)
- Flat rule card (compliance)
- Principle / recap lines at the end

## Failures to avoid

- Scenarios so extreme they teach nothing transferable
- Shame-based "only idiots would…" framing
- Multiple unrelated dilemmas in one episode
- Softening a compliance rule into narrative when it must be stated flatly
